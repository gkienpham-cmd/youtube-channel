THE MANNEQUIN WORLD (visual DNA — internalize before STATE 5)
================================================================================

Every visual in this clone channel follows a specific render language.

Default subjects: glossy white featureless mannequin figures with smooth eggshell
heads, no facial features, no eyes, no mouth, no nose, no ears. Bodies have
realistic human proportions and articulation but the surface is uniformly polished
plastic-like white.

Color-coded mannequins:
- Solid matte red — protagonist / focal subject. Full-body uniform red surface.
- Solid matte black — institutional antagonist (FBI, police, military). Full-body
  uniform black surface unless wearing photoreal tactical gear.
- Glossy white default — civilians, crowds, bystanders, unnamed roles.
- Photoreal clothing layered over white body — uniformed roles where the uniform
  conveys information (police with badges + duty belts, pilots with insignia,
  business suits where the suit IS the signal). Body underneath stays glossy white
  mannequin; clothing rendered photoreal.

Environments — two modes:
- Mode A — White void: infinite white seamless background, no horizon, no shadows
  except soft contact shadows. The void IS the environment.
- Mode B — Sparse realistic: specific photoreal location (airport hangar,
  courtroom, server room, apartment, subway car, mountain pass) with mannequins
  inserted. Props photoreal, mannequins still glossy white/red/black.

Render language: cinematic photorealistic 3D, Unreal Engine 5, octane render, 8K,
anamorphic lens, shot on ARRI Alexa, shallow depth of field, volumetric light,
dust particles in air, cinematic color grade.

Tone: clinical, observational, documentary. Never gory. Never horror-lit. Even
violent scenes rendered with restraint — implied through composition and lighting,
never depicted graphically.

This DNA is non-negotiable. The negative prompt in every chapter has to fight all
of this.


================================================================================
THE WRITING DNA (drill this — it's the whole game)
================================================================================

The script in STATE 3 must read as if it came from the source channel.

Cold open (first 3-4 sentences, ~30-40 words)
- A precise date (day + month + year, or month + year)
- A precise location (city, building, room — the more specific the better)
- A small concrete action or scene — a person doing a single specific thing, not
  abstract context

The cold open does NOT explain anything yet. It places the viewer inside a moment
and lets curiosity pull them forward. After the cold open, one anchor sentence
states what the video is about ("This is how it unfolded." / "This is the story of
[event].").

Sentence rhythm
- Short declarative sentences chained. 5-12 words is the sweet spot
- Present tense for narrative; past tense for context
- Occasional very short sentence for impact ("It works." / "Nobody answered.")
- Mix one longer expository sentence every 4-6 short ones
- Almost zero emotional adjectives in narrative beats. Adjectives appear only on
  physical descriptors (a 50-ton block, a dark tender area)
- Numbers and specifics over abstractions. Money in dollars. Distance in
  feet/meters. Time in precise units

Tone
- Deadpan, controlled, observational. Calm even when describing horror
- Direct viewer address sparse — used at structural pivots only
- Mild irony permitted on absurd facts. Never sarcasm. Never editorializing
- One striking line per ~200 words is allowed

Structural moves
- Cold open → context expansion → mechanism → implication
- Rhetorical questions as pivots between acts
- Lists of three when listing examples. Two feels incomplete. Four feels like
  padding
- The seemingly tangential detail that turns out to be load-bearing

Cliffhanger endings (MANDATORY)
Every script ends on one pattern from this list. Final 2-4 sentences (~30-50
words). Final line <12 words.
1. Unresolved present — situation in script is not over; something taken down is
   back, or never really ended
2. Implicating turn — pivot from script's specific subjects to viewer / society at
   large
3. Next mystery tease — name a related unsolved question or follow-on event
4. Unanswered question — close on a direct question the script raised but did not
   answer
5. Lingering image — return to the cold open's location/image, now charged with
   new meaning

Final line ends on a noun, name, date, or short declarative — never a clause that
resolves cleanly.

What to never do
- Never open with "In this video..." / "Today we're going to talk about..." /
  "Have you ever wondered..."
- Never explain what the script is about in the first 30 seconds
- Never use clickbait language ("INSANE," "you won't believe," "shocking truth")
- Never editorialize emotionally ("tragically," "horrifically," "devastatingly")
- Never include sponsor copy, subscribe prompts, or sign-offs
- Never end with a clean resolution — every script ends on a cliffhanger

System flow

- STATE 1 — Channel Branding (auto-generated from PDF)
- STATE 2 — Video Ideas (auto-generated from absorbed style)
- STATE 3 — Script Length + Continuous-Narration Script with cliffhanger

Chapter splitting
Seedance 2.0 generates up to 15 seconds per clip. Splits:

    Script duration  |  Total chapters  |  Per-chapter target
    ---------------- | ---------------- | --------------------
    60-75s           |  4-5             |  ~14-15s each
    90s              |  6               |  ~14-15s each
    2 min            |  8               |  ~14-15s each
    3 min            |  12              |  ~14-15s each
    5 min            |  20              |  ~14-15s each
    10 min           |  40              |  ~14-15s each

15s is the absolute model ceiling — never exceed.

Beat density and match-cut rhythm WITHIN a chapter
Each 15s chapter contains 6-9 beats with mixed durations to create a percussive
match-cut rhythm:
- ANCHOR beats (~2-3s) — the protagonist or main subject in extended action.
  Story-carrying. These hold the narrative thread
- CUTAWAY beats (~1-1.5s) — quick intercuts to a detail, prop, environment, or
  parallel action. Texture beats. These create rhythm
Alternate them. Don't write a chapter that's all 2s beats — that reads as a
slideshow. Mix 2s + 1s + 2s + 1s + 2s + 1s + 3s for example. The final beat is
often slightly longer (2-3s) to land the chapter.

Beat naming convention: CATEGORY — TAG. The category signals beat type, the tag is
the specific moment.
For documentary-mannequin clones, useful categories include:
- WIDE — [scene] (establishing anchor)
- BEAT — [action] (character anchor)
- INSERT — [object] (cutaway to a prop)
- CUT — [detail] (cutaway to a tight detail)
- TIGHT — [face/hand] (character cutaway)
- RACK — [focus pull] (transitional)
Or anything that fits the chapter — the categories aren't fixed, they're a
vocabulary. The point is each beat name signals scope and purpose at a glance.

Match-cut handoffs BETWEEN chapters (mandatory)
Last 0.5-1s of Chapter N must visually match first 0.5-1s of Chapter N+1. Three
patterns:
- Zoom-into-darkness — Chapter ends pushing into a dark area; Ch N+1 opens already
  inside, pulling back
- Match-cut on shape — Chapter ends on a circle/line/silhouette; Ch N+1 opens on
  the same shape at a different scale
- Whip-blur — Chapter ends with fast camera whip; Ch N+1 opens mid-whip and
  decelerates

Character description rules — FULL DESCRIPTION INLINE EVERY CHAPTER
CHARACTER LOCK (identical every beat) is one consolidated block at the top of every
chapter prompt. It contains the full creation-prompt prose from STATE 4 pasted
verbatim for every main character that appears in this chapter, plus reference
image filename for each.
Multi-character chapters list each character tagged inline within the same
CHARACTER LOCK block. The block is identical across every chapter that character
appears in — word-for-word.
This rigid template + reference image upload = zero room for character drift.
Background characters (no creation prompt) are described inline within the relevant
beat's visual paragraph using the same mannequin-world DNA — color, count, pose,
environment context.

Sound design — split into three tracks
Each beat's audio direction breaks into:
- SFX: specific event sounds (single-shot, attention-grabbing) — typewriter
  clicks, door slam, gunshot, cab horn, glass break
- Ambient: continuous environmental wash — room tone, traffic hum, office chatter,
  tiled echo, night city
- Music: post-score mood/arc reference — describes the intended emotional bed.
  Within a single 15s clip, music can have an arc (pulse begins → brass joins →
  resolves). Cross-chapter music doesn't blend — Seedance generates audio per-clip
  — so the user post-scores anyway. The Music: line is reference for that
  post-scoring
If a beat doesn't need one of the three, omit that line.

Camera direction integrated into visual paragraph
Camera moves are written inside the beat's visual description, not on a separate
line. Examples: "subtle dolly-in on the gasp," "cinematic side-angle," "tracking
shot following him through glass office doors," "low-angle street." The camera move
is part of how the beat is described, not metadata.
One camera move per beat. Stacked moves cause Seedance jitter.

Background / environment — folded into beats
No separate BACKGROUND LOCK block. Environment context lives inside each beat's
visual paragraph. If a chapter is single-location (e.g., FBI raid in an
apartment), every beat's visual paragraph describes that same apartment naturally.
If multi-location (montage), each beat describes its own location.
This handles both modes (Mode A white void / Mode B sparse realistic) flexibly
without a rigid lock that fights montage chapters.

Thumbnail DNA — Fern channel reference (from source PDF)
The Fern thumbnail style typically features:
- Composition: clean asymmetric layout. One dominant focal-subject mannequin
  (usually red protagonist) center or center-left. Heavy use of negative space and
  stark contrast
- Color palette: bold red, pure white, deep black. The red mannequin protagonist
  against stark institutional grey or white. Occasional use of high-contrast
  environments (Mode B sparse realistic) like government buildings, courtrooms,
  military backdrops
- Text treatment: short, bold, all-caps sans-serif label words. Often a single
  high-impact word in a red rectangular label tag (e.g. "SPY," "HUNTED,"
  "TRAITOR," "MOLE") with a small triangular pointer indicator pointing AT the
  focal mannequin's head. White or red text. Minimal copy — usually 1-3 words
- Focal-subject framing: medium to medium-wide shot of the protagonist mannequin
  in the institutional environment, surrounded by background mannequins (white
  default crowds) to emphasize isolation or being singled out. Or a tight chest-up
  of the red mannequin with environmental cues behind
- Emotion triggers: isolation, paranoia, exposure, being watched, the moment of
  identification. The thumbnail visually says "this person is the one."
- Lighting: cinematic key light from above-right, soft contact shadows, volumetric
  atmosphere matching the animation render language
