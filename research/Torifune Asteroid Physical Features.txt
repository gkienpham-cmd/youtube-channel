Procedural Generation Specification for Asteroid (98943) Torifune
Executive Summary
The near-Earth asteroid (98943) Torifune, provisionally designated 2001 CC21, serves as the primary flyby target for the Japan Aerospace Exploration Agency's (JAXA) Hayabusa2 Extended Mission (Hayabusa2#) scheduled for July 5, 20261. Translating the physical, kinetic, and optical properties of this celestial body into a physically based 3D rendering pipeline requires synthesizing comprehensive astronomical data—ranging from thermophysical models to spectro-photometric observations.
The analytical consensus indicates that Torifune is a relatively bright, highly elongated, stony S-complex (Sq-type) asteroid with a broadly homogeneous surface composition3. It exhibits a stable, prograde rotation with a nearly upright orientation relative to the ecliptic plane4. The following specification details the exact geometric parameters, topographical displacements, material shading properties, and environmental encounter dynamics required to generate a scientifically accurate, procedural 3D model of Torifune suitable for a programmatic environment.

	Surface Topography and Regolith Displacement
The surface topology of Torifune is shaped by its mineralogy, lack of strong gravitational retention, and continuous exposure to the space environment.
Spectro-photometric observations obtained across complete rotational phases confirm a lack of detectable large-scale heterogeneity4. This indicates that the 3D displacement map should be procedurally uniform, avoiding isolated regions of distinct mineralogy or singular, massive impact basins. Thermophysical models estimate a crater fraction of approximately 0.514, suggesting a heavily pockmarked but broadly consistent surface7.
As an object under one kilometer in diameter, Torifune's low surface gravity prevents the retention of fine dust, leading to a surface dominated by coarse regolith and rocky boulders, characteristic of S-type near-Earth objects like Itokawa9. The spectral data further indicate moderate space weathering, meaning the surface features are physically mature3. Within a procedural displacement pipeline, this translates to utilizing low-frequency noise algorithms (such as Musgrave or Perlin) to generate shallow, highly degraded crater rims, overlaid with high-frequency cellular noise to scatter angular boulders ranging from one to twenty meters in diameter across the regolith pools.
Material Shading and Photometric Properties
The material shader parameters must precisely reflect the spectro-photometric data associated with an Sq-type silicate asteroid to achieve photorealism.
Albedo and Reflectance
Torifune is unusually reflective for a near-Earth object. Although previously misclassified as a calcium-aluminum-rich L-type asteroid, extensive spectroscopic observation confirms it is an Sq-type object2. Its surface consists primarily of silicate minerals—approximately 60% olivine and 40% pyroxene—correlating closely with LL/L ordinary chondrite meteorites3.
This composition yields a geometric albedo ranging from    to   , with some thermophysical models suggesting absolute limits up to 0.405 under specific phase angles6. For a physically based rendering (PBR) shader, the base color luminosity must be strictly calibrated to reflect approximately 22% of incident light, completely avoiding the dark, carbonaceous tones typical of C-type asteroids like Ryugu3. Furthermore, as a heavily weathered silicate rock, the material lacks macroscopic metallic reflections, requiring a specular value near zero and a roughness value approaching 1.0 to account for light scattering within the porous regolith7.

	Kinematics and Scene Animation
The rotational dynamics of Torifune dictate the animation timeline and the progression of terminator lines across the surface mesh.
The asteroid is a stable, fast rotator, completing a full revolution every    hours3. Telescopic monitoring shows no evidence of a tumbling mode or non-principal axis rotation, permitting a simple, single-axis rotation constraint in the 3D scene16.

Encounter Dynamics and Illumination Geometry

Solar Irradiance
At the time of the closest approach, Torifune will be near its perihelion at a distance of 0.81 Astronomical Units (AU) from the Sun2. According to the inverse-square law of light, solar irradiance at this distance is significantly higher than at Earth orbit (  ). The directional light source simulating the Sun must be scaled to approximately 1.52 times the standard solar intensity. Camera Animation and Trajectory
The flyby involves extreme relative velocities. The spacecraft will streak past the asteroid at   , with a targeted closest approach distance between 1 and 10 kilometers from the asteroid's center to mitigate collision risks2.
Hayabusa2# was engineered for rendezvous operations rather than high-speed flybys; thus, its Optical Navigation Camera (ONC-T) is fixed to the main spacecraft body1. Rapidly altering the spacecraft's attitude to track the asteroid at 5.25 km/s is mechanically restrictive. Therefore, the mission profile relies on high-precision navigation to maintain a fixed orientation, capturing the asteroid as it traverses the field of view2. To accurately replicate this in a 3D environment, the virtual camera must be animated along a linear spline at a constant relative speed of 5.25 km/s, passing the asteroid mesh at a minimum offset of 1 to 10 kilometers, utilizing zero gimbal tracking to emulate the fixed-attitude constraints of the probe.
