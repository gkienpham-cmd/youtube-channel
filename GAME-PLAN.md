## 0. The thesis in one paragraph

You're not starting a "science channel" or a "history channel." You're starting a **curiosity-gap channel**: every video takes a word or event the viewer already half-knows — *quantum entanglement, the 2011 Japan tsunami, black holes, GPS* — and reveals the staggering depth underneath it, cinematically, in ~12–15 minutes, in your own human voice. The promise is identical every time: **"You've heard of this. You have no idea how deep it goes."** That consistency is what trains the algorithm and builds a brand, even while your *topics* roam across science, space, nature, and hidden history.

## 2. Your niche & positioning

### The promise (your one-sentence identity)
> **"Things you've heard of but never truly understood — explained like a film."**

Every title, thumbnail, and hook is a variation on *"you know the name; you don't know the story."*

### The lens (what makes a topic "yours")
A topic qualifies if it passes all four:
1. **Familiar** — the viewer recognizes the term (quantum entanglement, the Mariana Trench, the Carrington Event).
2. **Under-explored** — they've never had it explained properly.
3. **Visually rich** — it can be shown, not just told (essential for cinematic style).
4. **Awe-positive & all-ages** — it ends in wonder, not dread.

### Your differentiator: **awe, not dread** 🌍
Much of the cinematic-faceless space (Fern, Blackfiles, and the whole "dark/mystery" lane) trades on darkness and mature shock. The research *confirmed* Fern skews too mature for younger viewers. **You own the opposite lane:** the goosebumps-of-wonder, "the universe is stranger and more beautiful than they told you," genuinely-watchable-with-your-kid lane. Think the optimism of Kurzgesagt with the cinematic texture of Fern and a real human voice. Almost nobody is executing *that* exact combination. **That is your wedge.**

### Launch lane → expansion lanes
Don't boil the ocean on day one. The algorithm needs to learn who to show you to.

- **Launch lane (videos 1–~15): the natural & physical world.** Physics concepts, space, oceans, natural disasters, "how reality works." This is where *both* your examples (quantum entanglement, the Japan tsunami) live. It's evergreen, maximally visual, all-ages, and has huge existing search demand.
- **Expansion lane A (once established): how the world really works** — systems, infrastructure, economics-as-curiosity ("why does X exist").
- **Expansion lane B: hidden history & mysteries** — but always through the awe-not-dread lens (lost technology, the Antikythera mechanism, archaeological puzzles), never true-crime grimness.

## 3. The cadence verdict — your 3×/week question, answered honestly

**Short answer: 3 cinematic long-form essays per week solo is the #1 way you will burn out and watch your quality collapse.** The research's hardest fact proves it: *Fern, a studio of ~100 people, uploads once a week.* You are one person. Matching a studio's quality at 3× a studio's volume is not a stretch goal — it's impossible, and trying will produce exactly the mediocre output you said you want to avoid.

**The realistic math for one genuinely cinematic 12–15 min essay, solo:**

| Stage | Hours |
|---|---|
| Research + primary-source verification | 4–7 |
| Scripting + rewriting for voice | 5–9 |
| Narration + re-records | 1–2 |
| Sourcing/generating visuals (footage, AI stills, motion gfx) | 8–14 |
| Editing in Premiere | 8–14 |
| Thumbnail + title + packaging | 2–4 |
| Cutting 3 shorts from it | 2–4 |
| **Total** | **~30–50 hrs = one video** |

At your 30+ hrs/week, that's **one high-quality long-form per week** once your systems are dialed in (slower at first). That's not a compromise — **that's literally matching a 100-person studio's cadence as a solo creator.** Be proud of it.

### The reframe that keeps your "3× a week" instinct alive
You wanted to publish 3×/week. **You will — just not all long-form.** This is the model:

> **1 cinematic long-form per week** (the watch-time + revenue engine)
> **+ 3–5 vertical shorts per week** (the discovery + subscriber engine)

That's 4–6 publishes a week, satisfies the cadence instinct, *and* protects the quality. The shorts are mostly cut from the long-form (see §6), so they cost hours, not days.

**Launch tactic:** don't debut with one lonely video. **Bank 3 finished long-forms before you launch** and publish all three in week one (then weekly after). A channel with 3 strong videos invites a binge; a channel with 1 looks abandoned.

## 4. The packaging system — where new channels live or die

*(The research killed every specific CTR/retention number online, so there are no fake stats here — just the craft principles, which are sound. **[craft]**)*

### Package-first, always
Before you write a single line of script, write the **title** and sketch the **thumbnail**. If you can't make a compelling package, the topic isn't ready — *no matter how interesting the content is.* Design the video to deliver on the package, not the other way around.

### Title formulas (curiosity gap = familiar term + hidden stakes)
- *"[Familiar thing]: The [unexpected truth] Nobody Explains"* → "Quantum Entanglement: The 'Spooky' Link Einstein Refused to Believe"
- *"The [N] [Minutes/Seconds] That [huge consequence]"* → "The 6 Minutes That Redrew the Map of Japan"
- *"What's Actually [happening/inside/at the bottom of] [familiar thing]"* → "What's Actually at the Bottom of the Mariana Trench"
- *"Why [familiar everyday thing] Secretly Depends on [surprising thing]"* → "Why Your GPS Would Fail Without Einstein"

Keep it under ~60 characters so it doesn't truncate. One curiosity gap per title — don't stack two.

### Thumbnail rules
- **One subject, one emotion, one focal point.** Readable as a thumbnail-sized smudge.
- **High contrast + your signature color grade** so it's recognizably *yours* in a feed.
- **≤ 3–4 words of text**, huge. The title carries the words; the thumbnail carries the *feeling*.
- **Thumbnail and title must not repeat each other** — they should combine into a bigger question than either alone.
- Make the thumbnail *first*, at small size. If it doesn't stop you, redo it.

### The first 30 seconds (the hook) — your make-or-break
This format lives and dies in the cold open. A template that works:

1. **0:00–0:05 — The arresting image + line.** Open on your single most stunning visual and a sentence that *is* the curiosity gap. No logo, no "hey guys," no slow build. ("In 2011, the seafloor off Japan moved 50 meters sideways in under three minutes. Most people have no idea what that actually did.")
2. **0:05–0:15 — Name the gap.** Tell them what they *think* they know, then crack it open. ("You've heard it called a tsunami. That word is almost insultingly small for what really happened.")
3. **0:15–0:30 — Promise the payoff + open the loop.** Tell them the staggering thing they'll understand by the end — then *don't* resolve it yet. Roll your 3-second sting *after* this, not before.

Then structure the body as a chain of small open-and-closed loops so there's always a reason to keep watching, building to the awe payoff.

## 5. The production pipeline — your tool stack, verdict by stage

Your proposed stack is **mostly right.** Here's the stage-by-stage verdict, including the two places to adjust.

| Stage | Tool | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Ideation/research** | Claude + **primary sources** | ✅ Use Claude to map a topic & find threads — but verify every fact against real sources (see guardrail ⚠️). |
| **2. Script draft** | Claude | ✅ Great for structure, narrative flow, analogies, hook variations. ⚠️ See the guardrail below. |
| **3. Narration** | **Your voice → MP3** | ✅✅ Your single best decision. Human voice is now a *monetization moat* [verified]. Invest in a decent mic (e.g. an SM7B-class dynamic or a good large-diaphragm condenser) + a treated/quiet room. Pace it slow and warm. |
| **4. Visuals** | **Real footage + Gemini "Nano Banana" + Midjourney + motion gfx** | ✅ But **blend**, don't go all-AI (see below). |
| **5. Music & sound design** | Epidemic Sound / Artlist | ✅ Non-negotiable for "cinematic." Budget for it. |
| **6. Edit** | **Adobe Premiere** | ✅ Decisively. NOT Claude Code (see below). |
| **7. Captions** | Premiere auto-captions / Descript, then hand-fix | ✅ Always burn in or enable — huge for retention & accessibility. |
| **8. Thumbnail** | Photoshop/Photopea + Nano Banana for elements | ✅ |
| **9. Shorts** | Premiere or CapCut | ✅ See §6. |

### ⚠️ The one guardrail that protects your whole channel: fact-checking
You are building a brand on **teaching people the truth.** AI models — including me — will occasionally state a wrong date, a fabricated "study," or a confident-sounding false detail. **On an educational channel, one viral factual error in the comments can brand you as slop and undo months of trust.** So:

- Use Claude for **structure, story, clarity, and analogies** — its genuine strengths.
- Do **NOT** treat AI output as a source of facts. Every name, date, number, and "studies show" claim gets verified against a real primary/reputable source before it enters the script.
- Keep a sources list per video (also lets you cite them in the description — builds credibility).
- This verification pass is the 4–7 research hours in §3. It's not optional; it's the moat.

### Don't go 100% AI images — *blend* for credibility
Pure AI-image videos start to look samey and "off," and on factual topics they read as cheap. The cinematic-credibility recipe is a **layered** visual stack:
- **Real archival footage + photos** (NASA/ESA/USGS/public-domain/licensed) as the *factual backbone* — this is what makes it feel like a documentary, not a slideshow.
- **Gemini "Nano Banana" / Midjourney** for the *impossible or stylized* shots (a neutron star's surface, a cutaway of Earth's core, historical reconstructions).
- **Stock** (Storyblocks/Artgrid) for connective B-roll.
- **Motion graphics & data viz** (After Effects) for the *explaining* moments — diagrams, timelines, scale comparisons. This is often what separates "good" from "great" in this genre.

### The editing verdict: **Premiere, not Claude Code**
You're already comfortable in Premiere — that settles it. Real video editing is craft-by-feel: pacing, audio ducking under narration, J/L cuts, color, rhythm, knowing when to hold a shot. That lives in a timeline you can *feel*, not in a code agent. **Edit in Premiere.**

**But here's the smart division of labor — Claude Code *is* genuinely useful around the edit, just not *as* the editor:**
- Write & iterate scripts, hooks, title/thumbnail variants.
- Generate **ffmpeg** batch commands (transcode, normalize loudness, auto-cut shorts to vertical, strip watermarks).
- Generate/clean **SRT caption** files.
- Build **After Effects expressions** or **code-based motion graphics** (SVG/HTML animations, data-viz, kinetic typography) you render and import.
- Organize assets, write the publishing checklist, draft descriptions/tags/chapters, build your topic backlog.

So: **Claude Code = your writers' room + automation assistant. Premiere = your edit bay.** That's the optimal split.

## 6. The multiplatform engine (Shorts / Reels / TikTok)

**Principle: design *native* shorts, don't just chop the long-form. [craft]** A lazy 9:16 crop of a mid-video moment dies. A purpose-built short is a *self-contained* hook→payoff in 30–50s.

- **Two short types:** (a) the single most arresting 30–45s *extracted* from the long-form (the "whoa" beat), reframed vertical with big captions and a killer first frame; (b) purpose-built teaser/standalones on a sub-fact.
- **Post natively to each platform.** Upload separately to Shorts, Reels, and TikTok. **Strip the TikTok watermark** before posting to Reels/Shorts — platforms suppress competitor-watermarked clips.
- **First frame + first line are everything** — same hook discipline as §4, compressed.
- **Funnel honestly:** shorts are *top-of-funnel discovery and subscriber growth*; they do NOT meaningfully drive long-form watch time on their own. Use them to grow reach and subs, mention "full breakdown on the channel," and let the *long-form* do the watch-time and revenue work. Don't expect a short viewer to autoflow into a 15-min essay — treat them as two linked but distinct jobs.
- **Cadence:** 3–5/week, mostly milled from each weekly long-form, so one long-form feeds the whole week of shorts.

## 7. Monetization & growth roadmap

### The threshold (YouTube Partner Program)
**1,000 subscribers + 4,000 valid public watch-hours in the trailing 12 months**, *or* **1,000 subs + 10M valid Shorts views in 90 days.** (A lower 500-sub tier unlocks some fan-funding features earlier in eligible regions.)

### Realistic timeline — set expectations honestly
The research **refuted** every "monetized in 6 months" promise. The truth: **it's lumpy and unpredictable, and one breakout video matters more than twenty average ones.** Quality-first channels commonly take anywhere from a few months to 12–18 months to hit YPP, depending entirely on whether a video or two breaks out. **Don't optimize for speed-to-YPP** — ad money is small early anyway. Optimize for (a) a tight niche signal and (b) producing a few "hero" videos the algorithm wants to push.

### The money reality
- **Ads:** ~$5–$15 RPM take-home [verified] — modest. At 100k monthly views that's roughly $500–$1,500/mo. The floor, not the ceiling.
- **The real upside for *this* niche:** educational/explainer channels monetize disproportionately through **sponsorships** (Brilliant, Ground News, NordVPN-type, educational brands), **channel memberships**, and eventually an invite to a creator co-op like **Nebula**. Build toward those — they can dwarf ad revenue.

### Growth phases
- **0 → 1,000 subs:** Don't chase subs — chase *signal clarity* and *packaging reps*. Make 10–15 videos that unmistakably say "this is what we do." Reply to every comment (early engagement compounds). Expect non-linear growth; one video will likely outrun the rest 10:1 — when it does, **make three more like it.**
- **1,000 → 10,000:** Double down on proven winners. Turn your best video into a **series/playlist** ("Forces of Nature," "The Invisible Universe," "Things You Were Never Taught") to drive bingeing and identity. Tighten the intro sting, lock the brand look, start pitching small sponsors.
